# Biology - Exam Study Report

Pearson Edexcel International A Level Biology: International AS units WBI11-WBI13, A2 units WBI14-WBI16, and the older WBI01-WBI06 papers. Built from a topic-sorted bank of 3,132 past-paper question parts ("items") from 175 papers, sessions 2014-2024 (2025 papers are deliberately excluded). Every number here comes from that bank. Where the bank cannot answer a question, this report says so.

## What the paper codes mean

Pearson gives every paper a code such as **WBI11**.

- **W** means an International Advanced Level paper. **BI** means Biology.
- The **first digit** is the specification: **1** = current 2018 specification (WBI11-WBI16); **0** = previous 2013 specification (WBI01-WBI06).
- The **last digit** is the unit number. Units 1-3 are International AS; units 4-6 are A2.

| Unit | Name | Level | Content in this report |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Molecules, Diet, Transport and Health | AS | Topics 1 and 2 |
| 2 | Cells, Development, Biodiversity and Conservation | AS | Topics 3 and 4 |
| 3 | Practical Skills in Biology I | AS | P3 codes |
| 4 | Energy, Environment, Microbiology and Immunity | A2 | Topics 5 and 6 |
| 5 | Respiration, Internal Environment, Coordination and Gene Technology | A2 | Topics 7 and 8 |
| 6 | Practical Skills in Biology II | A2 | P6 codes |

An item ID such as **WBI12_Jun2021-Q7-b** reads: paper code (current spec, Unit 2), session (June 2021), question 7, part b. Spec codes such as **B3.4** mean topic 3, point 4 of the 2018 specification. **P3/P6** codes are practical-skills items; **G** is general data-handling skill.

*Identify 21, Determine 18, Deduce 10, Find 8, Estimate 8, Write 7, Evaluate 7, Sketch 5, Use 3, Plot 2, Outline, Predict, Analyse 1 each.

- **Explain, Describe and Suggest make up 1,805 items (57.6%)**: most marks reward written reasoning, not single-word recall.
- **Suggest** means applying biology to an unfamiliar case: a disease (PKU, galactosaemia, myeloma), an animal (penguins, camels, kangaroo rats), a drug or a poison. Schemes usually accept "any 3 of" several ideas, so give distinct points.
- "Comment" also appears in the sample notes (kidney data, lactate graphs, linkage) though it is not in the counted list.

### 3.2 One-mark and multiple-choice items

317 items (10%) are one-mark items, which the data groups as multiple choice. Some multiple-choice sets are stored as one item (six muscle-fibre questions under B7.11, five nucleotide questions under B2.9), so the exact number cannot be read off. Common forms: identify a labelled part on a diagram or electron micrograph; pick the bond (glycosidic, ester, peptide, hydrogen, phosphodiester); work out a base percentage (guanine 30%, so adenine 20%); "how many of these statements are correct".

### 3.3 Typical structure and data questions

Structured questions climb a ladder inside one context: stem (context, photo, diagram, data), short recall or multiple choice, "describe" the data, "explain" or "suggest", then a calculation, evaluation or starred answer. Example: a left-ventricle volume table asks for the cycle length (1), what happened to the semilunar valves (2), then a calculation (B1.8).

General data skills (G) give 159 items and 451 marks and appear every year: describe what a table or chart shows, with numbers. Data also drives many content items, such as B1.15 (health data, 32 items), B1.16 (study design, 14) and B1.18 (cholesterol data, 10). The data does not count all data-based items.

### 3.4 Scientific-article questions

The bank contains article questions whose notes cite paragraph numbers. In the samples they fall on Topic 5-8 points (B5.1, B5.25, B6.7, B7.1, B7.3, B7.14, B7.17, B7.21, B7.22, B8.2, B8.16, B8.17, B8.20, B8.21, B8.22). They ask you to explain an idea from the article with your own knowledge, or to "Discuss" a statement; some are starred 5-6 mark answers (Krebs cycle, B7.3; adrenaline secretion, B7.14; a transgenic sheep, B8.17). The data does not count them or name their paper, and several of their mark schemes were missing.

### 3.5 "Hence" bundles and prerequisites

36 items are "hence" bundles (a part building on the previous answer was merged with it), so practise them as one. 183 items (5.8%) list prerequisites; do those first. The samples show the usual chain: read a value, then use it (heart rate then cardiac output, B7.13; measure an image then calculate real size, B3.7; diversity index then which habitat is more diverse, B4.18). Error carried forward usually protects later marks if your method is right.

### 3.6 Starred extended-writing parts

Starred (*) parts are their own items, but the data gives no total. Two formats appear:

- **QWC points schemes (4-6 marks):** "any 6 of 9" marking points, with a focus on spelling of technical terms, logical sequence or clarity. Examples: transcription (B2.13), the rER-Golgi pathway (B3.4), mutation and enzymes (B2.14), natural selection (B5.23), thylakoid membranes (B5.3), splicing (B3.19), gel electrophoresis (B6.18), thermoregulation (B7.17).
- **Level-of-response grids (6 marks):** indicative content in bands worth 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6 marks, usually "use the information and your own knowledge". Samples: B1.9, B2.18, B4.11, B5.26, B6.12, B7.14.

"Describe an investigation" is a frequent starred task, even in theory papers (B1.14, B2.8, B3.15, B5.22, B6.14, B6.20, B7.8).

### 4.2 Linked steps

Most 3-6 mark Explain and Describe items are marked as chains, one mark per link:

- **Mutation to enzyme:** base change, different codons, different amino acid sequence and R groups, different folding, changed active site, substrate no longer fits (B2.14).
- **Protein secretion:** transcription, mRNA to ribosome, translation, folding in rER, vesicles, Golgi modification, secretory vesicles, exocytosis (B2.13, B3.4).
- **Specialisation:** stimulus, genes switched on or off, mRNA from active genes, protein modifies the cell (B3.18).
- **Selection and speciation:** variation by mutation, selection pressure, survivors breed and pass on alleles, allele frequency changes; plus isolation for speciation (B5.23, B5.24).
- **Homeostasis:** receptors, hypothalamus, effectors, negative feedback (B7.17, B7.21).
- **Impulse and synapse:** Na+ channels open, Na+ diffuses in; Ca2+ enters, vesicles fuse, transmitter released by exocytosis (B8.4, B8.6).
- **Genetic modification:** isolate gene, restriction endonuclease, vector, ligase, insert, culture (B8.17, B8.18).

Some marks count only as a pair ("thin wall gives a short diffusion distance", B1.7; "linked points must be paired", B5.3).

### 4.3 Data and comparisons

- State the trend, then add a **manipulated figure**: a difference, percentage or "times" change (B1.11, B1.15, B2.7). Name features such as plateau, optimum or inhibition (B8.11), with units (B3.13).
- In comparisons every point must cover both things ("each covering both processes", B3.10; "all points must be comparative", B5.13). Examiners will not piece together two separate descriptions (B4.11, B5.5, B6.5).

| Magnification and actual size | B3.7 |

- A correct answer alone usually earns full marks (B1.15, B4.15, B5.9), but show working: error carried forward rescues later marks (B3.7, B4.18, B6.20).
- Units: "no units, max 1 mark" (B7.20).
- Round as told: 2 s.f. (B7.14), 1 d.p. (B4.18, where 4.58 instead of 4.6 lost a mark), 2 d.p. (B4.20), 3 s.f. (B7.6).
- Make it sensible: 2.94 species earns 1 of 2; the answer is 3 (B4.17). Never take logs of logs (B6.3).

### 4.5 Level of response and QWC

- **Points-based QWC:** marks come from valid points; write in the order events happen and spell technical terms correctly.
- **Level of response:** in the samples, the level depends on using both graphs (B1.9), how many graphs you comment on (B7.14), how many aspects you explain and how fully (B5.26), and describing data alone stays in the bottom level (B2.18). Describe with figures, explain with biology, reach a judgement.

| Code | What it covers | Items | Marks | Share of unit | Marks per item |

| P6.analysis | Validity, data handling, statistics, limitations | 123 | 654 | 50.5% | 5.3 |
| P6.planning | Planning an investigation | 121 | 577 | 44.5% | 4.8 |
| P6.implementation | Short calculations | 25 | 65 | 5.0% | 2.6 |

**Unit 3 (results are over half the marks):**
- **Graphs:** independent variable on x, labels with units, accurate plotting, ruled line point to point or best fit, never freehand or extrapolated.
- **Planning:** name the independent variable; say how the dependent variable is measured (e.g. colorimeter); give each control variable a value and a method (thermostatic water bath, buffer); hazard plus precaution pairs.
- **Implementation:** fix a weak method (counting duckweed plants of different sizes, so measure mass or area at set times); write a **full reference**: authors, date, title, journal, volume, part, page, in order, with no extras such as "Vol.".

**Unit 6 (analysis and planning split almost evenly):**
- **Planning:** safety and ethics (animal welfare, gloves, harmful bacteria); preliminary work, where each mark needs the idea of finding a suitable value; enough individuals to cover variation.
- **Analysis:** why a conclusion is not valid (name uncontrolled variables such as age, sex, body mass); a results table with headings and units, a suitable graph, means and a **named statistical test** (t-test, Mann-Whitney U, chi-squared); limitations. The later planning sections on data handling and limitations are filed under P6.analysis.
- **Implementation:** e.g. mitotic index 41/(41+121) x 100 = 25.3.

**Recent shift:** 32 of 41 P3.implementation items and 23 of 25 P6.implementation items are from 2021-2024.

**Core practicals in theory papers:** B-coded core-practical items total 79 items and 368 marks. "Describe an investigation" schemes repeat one checklist: at least five values of the independent variable, how it is controlled, how the dependent variable is measured, named variables kept the same, a stated time, repeats and a mean (B2.8, B5.8, B5.22, B6.20, B7.8).

## 7. Suggested study plan

1. **Split time by marks.** For each unit you sit, divide revision roughly as its marks divide (section 1): even for Units 1 and 2, slightly more on Topic 5 than 6 and on Topic 7 than 8.
2. **Core points first.** Work every bank item on your unit's Core points, starting with top-25 and every-year points. Learn the exact terms (4.1) and chains (4.2).
3. **Next points, then recent Light points.** Do every item on B1.9, B3.16, B4.18, B4.20, B5.26, B6.3, B7.6, B7.20, B8.20 and B8.21; skim the other Light points.
4. **Weekly skill circuits:** G data items (trend plus manipulated figure), a calculation set (4.4), and one starred answer marked strictly against its scheme.
5. **Practical paper:** Unit 3 graphs, controls, hazards and references; Unit 6 full plans with a named statistical test and limitations.
6. **Timed papers:** 2021-2024 give 18 papers a year; start with your unit's latest sessions. Old WBI0x papers make extra topic practice.

| Practice route | What to pull from the bank | Builds |
|---|---|---|
| Spec-point drill | All items for one code, oldest to newest | Content, and how ideas get reworded |
| Command-word drill | "Suggest" and "Explain" items for your unit | Applying knowledge to new contexts |

| Data circuit | G items (159) and "describe the effect" parts | Trend plus figures |

| Practical set | P3 or P6 items, recent first | Graphs, planning, references, statistics |
| Full papers | One paper code, latest session first | Timing |

Do each "hence" bundle in one sitting, and clear an item's prerequisites before it.

## 8. Data caveats

- **Older papers:** WBI01-WBI06 questions were mapped to the closest 2018 spec point. The data does not split counts by specification, and it lists no OFF (off-spec) items: all 3,132 items sit under G, a P code or a 2018 B code.
- **Missing paper:** WBI06 June 2014 is a scanned paper that could not be read, so 2014 Unit 6 counts are short (P6.analysis and P6.planning have 4 and 5 items that year).
- **Mark schemes:** some in the source files were missing, belonged to another paper, or were examiners' reports. Notes flag this ("mark scheme not in input", "supplied MS covers only Q7(g)-(k)"), and in those cases the expected answer is inferred.
- **Splitting:** a few questions were split imperfectly: some sub-parts appear only in the mark scheme, some items cover a whole question, and at least one recall question (pectin production) sits under P6.implementation.
- **Counting limits:** command words are the first match in each description (387 items had none). Multiple-choice sets stored as one item make one-mark counts inexact. Starred and article items are not counted.
- **Years:** counts follow paper numbers (9 papers in 2016, 22 in 2019), so a one-year spike is not a trend. "Years seen" means at least one item that year.
- **Units:** papers per unit are not given, so totals mix how often a topic is tested with how many papers of its unit the bank holds.
- **Core practicals** examined in Units 3 and 6 are filed under P codes, so their B-code counts understate them.
- **2025** papers are excluded on purpose.
