# Maths - Exam Study Report

**Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics**
Pure P1-P4, Further Pure FP1-FP3, Mechanics M1-M3, Statistics S1-S3, Decision D1

This report comes from a topic-sorted bank of 6,204 past-paper question parts taken from 314 papers, sessions 2014 to 2024. Papers from 2025 and 2026 were left out on purpose. Every count and percentage here is taken from the bank or calculated from it. Where the bank cannot answer a question, the report says so.

---

## What the paper codes mean

Pearson gives every paper a code such as **WMA11**.

- **W** means an International Advanced Level paper.
- The two letters name the strand: **MA** Pure Mathematics, **FM** Further Pure Mathematics, **ME** Mechanics, **ST** Statistics, **DM** Decision Mathematics.
- The two digits give the unit within that strand: WMA11 = Pure Mathematics 1 (P1), WMA12 = P2, WMA13 = P3, WMA14 = P4; WFM01-03 = FP1-FP3; WME01-03 = M1-M3; WST01-03 = S1-S3; WDM11 = D1.
- WMA01, WMA02 and WDM01 are the earlier versions of P1, P2 and D1 from the previous specification.

Item IDs such as **WMA12_Jun2021-Q7-b** give the paper code, session, question and part: P2, June 2021, question 7, part (b). Spec point codes such as **P1.4.2** give the unit, topic and point: Pure 1, topic 4 (Differentiation), point 2. **G** marks general skills. **OFF** marks content that is not on the 2018 specification.

**Prove**, **Solve** and **Hence** are not counted as command words in the data, so their frequency is unknown. They appear often in the sample notes ("prove by induction", "solve ... to 2 d.p.", and the hence parts in 3.3). The words were matched in summaries written when the bank was built, not in the printed papers, and only the first word counts. "Show that ... hence find" counts once, as Show that.

**What the words mean for marks** (from the sample notes):

- **Find / Calculate / Determine:** show working. A few answers alone score in full (a circle's centre). Many score nothing alone: a Newton-Raphson step, a bin-packing lower bound, an integral where calculus is required, cubic roots with no algebra.
- **Show that:** the answer is printed, so the last mark is A1\*. You need every step and a closing statement, for example f(3) = 0 seen before concluding p = -87.
- **State / Write down:** usually one B1, no working, but the exact word matters ("discrete uniform", not "continuous uniform").
- **Sketch:** shape plus labelled intercepts, stationary points and asymptote equations. Without a sketch, marks are capped or lost.
- **Explain / Comment:** B1 for a reason, then often a dependent B1 for the conclusion, in context.
- **Use [named method]:** the method is fixed. Graphical or numerical methods are often refused, and starting Prim's algorithm at the wrong vertex costs most of the marks.
- **Hence:** use the previous result. One FP2 scheme refuses the formula-book Maclaurin series because the part says "Hence". "Hence or otherwise" allows another method.

### 3.2 Part structures

- **Short parts:** 531 items (9%) are worth one mark. The samples are State or Write down steps, such as giving the weight of a spanning tree after building it.
- **Typical shape:** a short set-up part, a main method part, then a part that changes a condition or applies the result. Examples from the bank:
  - P2.7.1: show that k = -78 → nature of the point from d²y/dx² → hence the stationary point.
  - D1.3.1: route inspection → route length → a new pipe is added: is the route longer or shorter?
  - S1.3.2: Venn diagram → which events are mutually exclusive → a conditional probability.
  - FP2.2.1: partial fractions → hence method of differences → hence sum to infinity.
  - FP3.6.7 / FP3.6.8: k from an eigenvalue → other eigenvalues → normalised eigenvector → diagonalise.
  - M2.4.2 / M2.4.3: speeds after impact in terms of e → range of e for a reversal or a second collision.

### 3.3 How many parts build on earlier ones

- 2,162 items (34.8%) list an earlier part as a prerequisite.
- 635 items (10.2%) are bundled "hence" items, where a hence part is stored with the part before it.
- The data does not say how much these groups overlap, so between 35% and 45% of all items depend on an earlier answer.
- Follow-through (ft) often protects you after an earlier mistake, but not always. Some notes make a mark depend on both earlier M marks, or on full marks in the first part.

## 4. What mark schemes reward

### 4.1 The codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| M1 | Method mark: a correct approach, even with your own numbers |
| A1 | Accuracy mark: only available if the M mark it depends on is earned |
| B1 | Independent mark for a correct value or statement |
| dM1 / DM1 | Dependent method mark: needs an earlier M mark |
| ft | Follow-through: credit for correct use of your own earlier answer |
| awrt | "Answers which round to": awrt 0.405 accepts anything that rounds to 0.405 |
| cao / cso | Correct answer only / correct solution only (no errors anywhere) |
| A1\* | Printed answer reached with full working and no errors |
| isw | Ignore subsequent working (some schemes say "no isw", so changing a correct answer loses the mark) |
| A2 (-1 each error) | Two accuracy marks for an equation, one lost per error (mostly mechanics) |
| SC | Special case: partial credit for a named common route |

### 4.2 Method versus accuracy

The bank does not count M, A and B marks separately, so no numerical split can be given. The sample schemes follow a consistent pattern:

- **Method marks forgive slips.** Notes allow one slip, condone sin/cos confusion or sign slips, and accept an equation with the correct number of terms that is dimensionally consistent.
- **Accuracy marks are strict about the final form.** 0.714 is refused where an exact fraction is needed. Degrees are refused where radians are asked for. ±3 is refused where k = 3. Integer coefficients, "y = mx + c" form and "a vector, not coordinates" are all enforced.
- **Dependent marks chain.** An early wrong method can remove several later marks. In one Dijkstra question, the DM1 in part (b) needs the M mark from part (a).

### 4.3 Accepted alternatives

- **Equivalent methods score the same.** Examples: energy or suvat for a projectile's speed; a whole-system equation for connected particles; trial and improvement for a geometric-series year (both neighbouring terms must be shown); adding 11 terms instead of using the sum formula; the cover-up rule for partial fractions; the factor theorem for quartic roots; writing 2ˣ as e^(x ln 2); several ways of splitting a compound area.
- **Some methods are refused.** Examples: factorising a quadratic with surd roots; arcsin where arsinh is needed; v dv/dx when acceleration is a function of t; series formulas for a periodic sequence; explicit complex roots when sum and product of roots are asked for; induction where standard sums are asked for.

### 4.4 Where marks are typically lost

| Area | Examples from the sample notes |
|---|---|
| Rounding | Regression coefficients rounded to 3 s.f. give 459, not 458 (A0); a rounded k = 6.33 gives A0 |
| Exact form | Decimals where exact values are needed; r = ±4; a focus written as (±3, 0) |
| Solutions | Extra solutions in range; not rejecting an invalid root (x = -1/2 in a log equation, v = 3, p = -2/3) |
| Inequalities | "and" between two separate regions; ≤ instead of <; region inequalities joined with "or" |
| Order | Composite functions in the wrong order; matrix product order (C = BA); impact law the wrong way round |
| Constants and units | Missing + c; not dividing by the inner derivative; kW not converted to W; g = 9.81 instead of 9.8; grams not converted to kg |
| Misreads | n instead of n - 1 in a geometric term; 2¹² used as the geometric sum; displacement given instead of distance; Var(3R) used where Var(R₁ + R₂ + R₃) is needed |
| Hypothesis tests | A probability given instead of a critical region; the wrong number of tails; a conclusion without context |
| Statistics wording | "Negative relationship" for correlation; "outlier" instead of extrapolation; "skew" missing from a skewness answer; "cause" or "causation" missing from a correlation comment |
| D1 presentation | Nodes listed instead of arcs; Kruskal rejections not shown; Dijkstra working values out of order; feasible region not labelled R; quick-sort pivots unclear |
| Mechanics | No direction for a velocity after impact; a speed left negative; "left" or "changed" instead of a direction |

## 5. How the strands differ

| Strand | Marks per item | Largest topic | What stands out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure | 4.3 | P1 algebra and functions (42.8% of P1); P4 integration (42.0% of P4) | Show-that parts, exact answers, algebra required |
| Further Pure | 4.9 | FP3 integration (34.3% of FP3) | Long proofs and derivations; many codes with few items each |
| Mechanics | 4.7 | M1 dynamics (32.8%); M3 circular motion (30.7%) | Equations from diagrams, A2 (-1 each error), g = 9.8 |
| Statistics | 3.6 | S3 estimation and tests (35.0%) | Short parts; wording and context marked; one hypothesis-test pattern |

**Pure.** Algebra carries everything else: it is 42.8% of P1 and 26.6% of P3. Trig equations (P2.6.2, 7.6 marks per item) and compound angles (P3.2.3, 6.6) are long. Schemes often refuse graphical or numerical methods and set a required accuracy.

**Further Pure.** FP2 has the most marks per item of any unit (6.6). Each code averages about 11 items, so you need breadth. Proofs and derivations score heavily: induction averages 8.7 marks per item and reduction formulae 9.3. Schemes often require calculus and refuse calculator-only answers. The bank has no FP2 or FP3 items from 2015 or 2016, so these counts rest on fewer sessions.

**Mechanics.** M1.3.1 is the most frequent point in the whole bank (132 items). Almost every mark comes from writing an equation: resolving, Newton's second law, moments, energy or momentum. Answers after using g = 9.8 are accepted to 2 or 3 significant figures (12.8 or 13 m; 15.7 or 16 m/s). M1.1.1 modelling gives 17 marks over 16 items, all short "how did you use the model" parts.

**Statistics.** S1 has the shortest parts in the bank (3.1 marks). State, Explain and Comment parts are marked on exact wording. Hypothesis tests follow one pattern:

1. B1 for hypotheses stated with the parameter.
2. M1/A1 for a probability or critical region.
3. dM1 for a comment that does not contradict itself.
4. A1 cso for a conclusion in context.

Table values recur (z = 1.6449, 2.3263, 2.5758). Several S2 items have no mark scheme in the bank, so S2 marking notes are thinner.

**Decision.** D1 has the most marks per item of any strand (5.1) and 7 of the top 25 points. Marks reward carrying out the algorithm visibly and in order: arcs in the order selected, the order of labelling, every rejection, clear pivots. Answers go on printed diagrams. Matchings (19 items, 2014-2019) are no longer on the specification.

## 7. Suggested study plan

### 7.1 The cycle for each unit

1. **Start with the must-know list** from section 6.
2. **Begin each point with its shortest parts.** Do one- and two-mark items first, then 3-5 mark parts, then whole questions. Short parts drill the facts and notation that long parts assume; the bank has 531 one-mark items to start from.
3. **Work every "hence" chain as one piece.** The 635 bundled hence items are stored with the part before them. Do and mark them together, because dependent and cso marks in the hence part rely on the earlier one.
4. **Do prerequisites first.** For the 2,162 items that list an earlier part, do that part first, as it appears on the paper.
5. **Mark with the scheme every time.** File each lost mark under a heading from section 4.4. Headings that keep recurring are your real weak spots.
6. **Move to the high tier.** Then do timed mixed sets from the most recent sessions; 2021-2024 supply 150 of the 314 papers.
7. **Finish with medium and low points:** one worked example and one short drill each. Treat the recent-only points (P3.3.3, P4.1.1, P2.1.2, P1.1.3, P4.5.2, D1.3.3) as at least medium.

A workable week for one unit has five sessions:

- two sessions on must-know points;
- one session on their hence chains, timed;
- one session on a high-tier point;
- one timed mixed set followed by an error-log review.

### 7.2 Practice routes across units

These routes follow how topics build on each other. They are suggestions only: the bank does not record links between units.

- **Differentiation:** P1.4.2 → P1.4.3 → P2.7.1 → P3.4.2 → P4.5.1 → FP3.3.2

## 8. Data caveats

1. **Sessions.** Only 2014-2024 are included. 2025 and 2026 papers were deliberately excluded.
2. **Spec mapping.** Older papers, including WMA01, WMA02 and WDM01, were mapped to the closest 2018 spec point. The 19 items with no 2018 equivalent are tagged OFF; the samples shown are D1 matchings from 2014-2019.
3. **Unreadable papers.** Six papers could not be read: WME01 Jun 2014, WFM02 Jun 2015, WFM03 Jun 2015, WFM02 Jun 2019, WME03 Jun 2019 and WST02 Jun 2019. They are missing from every count.
4. **Garbled symbols.** Maths symbols in the source text were often garbled, so some question summaries were rebuilt from the mark schemes. Many notes say a value was "lost" or "reconstructed from the scheme".
5. **Missing stems.** Several Question 1 stems were missing from the text.
6. **Missing or mismatched mark schemes.** Some mark schemes were missing; several S2 items carry only the printed mark allocation. Others belonged to another paper (some S3 items) or to a reserve paper (some P4 items).
7. **D1 answer-book pages.** These appear as extra items, for example under D1.4.3, D1.4.4, D1.2.2 and D1.3.3. D1 item counts are therefore inflated. The data does not say by how many, or whether their marks are counted again.
8. **No S1 Normal code.** The spec parser missed the S1 Normal distribution, so Normal-distribution questions were coded to S2.3.2, the nearest S2 point. S1 is understated and S2 overstated by an unknown amount.
9. **Topic-list quirks.** FP2.7.1 covers both polar coordinates and polar area. Friction appears twice in M1 (M1.4.4 and M1.5.3). Some titles are cut short or scrambled (for example P3.4.3, M3.4.1 and M3.4.2). Nine codes have no items (section 2.2).
10. **Uneven coverage.** Papers per year range from 19 (2014) to 42 (2021), and papers per unit are not given. Counts per year or per unit are not like-for-like. "Years seen" is the safest measure of how regularly a point is tested.
11. **Command words.** The counts come from item summaries and use only the first word. Prove, Solve and Hence are not counted.
12. **One-mark items.** The data labels the 531 one-mark items "multiple choice". The samples are short State or Write down parts, so treat the label with caution.
13. **Item boundaries.** Some items hold a whole question under one ID, and some parts never got their own ID. Item counts therefore slightly understate the number of printed parts, and marks per item vary.
14. **What counts measure.** Counts show how often a point was coded. They do not measure difficulty, and a low count does not prove a skill is rarely needed.
