# Chemistry: Exam Study Report

Pearson Edexcel International A Level Chemistry: WCH11–WCH16 (2018 specification) plus the older WCH01–WCH07 papers.

This report is built from a topic-sorted bank of 176 past papers from 2014–2024. The 2025 papers were left out on purpose. The bank holds **4,662 items worth 12,248 marks**. An item is one question part, and linked "hence" parts are bundled into a single item. Every figure here comes from the bank data or is calculated from it. Section 8 explains what the data cannot tell you.

## 3. How questions are asked

**Command words.** Each item was counted by the first command word in its "how asked" summary, and 2,684 items matched one: Explain 427 (15.9%), Calculate 414 (15.4%), State 386 (14.4%), Give 248 (9.2%), Identify 174 (6.5%), Suggest 159 (5.9%), Draw 158 (5.9%), Name 131 (4.9%), Describe 121 (4.5%), Find 118 (4.4%), Complete 89, Deduce 88, Write 44, Justify 23, Compare 22, Sketch 14, Determine 13, Use 11, Show that 11, Outline 9, Predict 9, Estimate 6, Plot 5, Evaluate 3, Discuss 1.

Grouped, the matched items split like this:

- **Short recall** (State, Give, Name, Identify): 35.0%.
- **Reasoning** (Explain, Suggest, Describe, Justify, Compare, Outline, Evaluate, Discuss): 28.5%.
- **Working out** (Calculate, Find, Determine, Estimate, Show that): 20.9%.
- **Drawing and writing out** (Draw, Complete, Write, Sketch, Plot): 11.5%.
- **Applying** (Deduce, Use, Predict): 4.0%.

The other 1,978 items matched no command word. Multiple-choice stems usually start "which…", so most of these are probably multiple-choice items.

**Multiple choice (58% of items).** These forms come up often in the notes:

- **Calculation trap options.** Wrong options come from a missed factor: options that "omit a factor of 2 or 10" (C16.3), Ecell multiplied by the stoichiometry (C16.7), or one chloride counted instead of two (C12.17).
- **"Which is not…" stems:** C3.4, C5.8, C7.3, C8.27, C17.29.
- **Linked sets** of questions on one context: C4.18, C7.2.
- **Graph reading:** Maxwell–Boltzmann curves (C9.4), zero-order graphs (C11.5), tangent against average rate (C9.3).

**Structured questions** come in six recurring shapes:

1. **Calculation chains**, with a mark for each line (C6.5, C12.8, C16.15, C20.1).
2. **Reaction schemes:** reagents, conditions and intermediates for each step (C5.4, C19.5, C20.3).
3. **Mechanisms**, worth up to 7 marks in C4.18 and 6 in C5.6.
4. **Identifying an unknown** from test results and spectra (C15.15, C20.3).
5. **Starred (*), QWC or levels-of-response answers**, from 2 marks (C2.8) up to 6–7 marks (C4.18, C7.1, C9.11, C15.3, C17.29).
6. **Large context items**, such as an 18-mark weak-base question (C14.15).

**"Hence" chains and prerequisites.** The bank has 79 bundled "hence" chains, and 200 items list prerequisites. The data gives these counts, not the lists. Examples are "(ii) hence moles HCl" (P3.results) and "(ii) hence complete the equation" (C14.8).

Error carried forward (ecf/TE) protects later steps. It appears in the notes for C1.7, C1.9, C6.10, C10.10, C12.7, C16.9, C20.1 and P3.results, so always show your method.

These links between topics are visible in the data:

- Moles (T1) feed titrations (C8.20, C16.15), calorimetry (C6.5), Kc (C13.3), Ka (C14.14), buffers (C14.19) and combustion analysis (C20.1).
- Electronic configurations (C2.14) lead to ionisation energy explanations (C2.10) and d-block configurations (C17.2).
- Polarisation (C3.9) leads to thermal stability (C8.16) and to covalent character in lattice energies (C12.14, C12.15).
- SN2 (C10.9) leads to rate evidence for SN1/SN2 (C11.9) and to optical activity evidence (C15.5).
- Entropy (T12) leads to ΔS total = R ln K (C13.8), Ecell and ln K (C16.11), and the chelate effect (C17.25).
- Electrode potentials (C16.10) lead to vanadium and chromium chemistry (C17.19, C17.20).

## 4. What mark schemes reward

These rules come from the sample mark-scheme notes. The code in brackets shows where each rule was seen.

**Calculations**
- **Significant figures are marked.** 0.417 g, "3 s.f. required" (P3.results); −495 kJ mol⁻¹, "value, then sign and 3 sf" (C6.5).
- **Over-rounding fails.** pH 12.7 is accepted and 13 is rejected (C14.11).
- **A correct answer alone often gets full marks** (C14.11, C16.9). After a slip, though, only shown working can earn ecf marks.
- **Factors are marked.** "factor 2" (C6.5), two chloride ions (C12.17), halving the moles for Mg(OH)₂ (P3.results).

**Signs and units**
- **ΔH needs its sign.** "+413 kJ/mol (sign matters)", while "−347 scores 1" (C6.10).
- **Entropy units.** Convert kJ to J in ΔS surroundings = −ΔH/T, and give ΔS in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ (C12.8).
- **Work units out from the expression.** dm³ mol⁻¹ (C13.3), atm⁴ (C13.2), s⁻¹ (C11.1); wrong Ka units are "penalised" (C14.14).
- **Oxidation numbers need a sign.** "A bare '2' … is not accepted" (C8.2).

**Equations and state symbols**
- **The usual split** is 1 mark for correct formulae and 1 for balancing and/or state symbols (C2.8, C8.13, C8.19, C10.17).
- **Missing state symbols cap you at 1 mark** (C4.17). The state-symbol mark needs a correct equation (C8.14).
- **Ionic equations:** no spectator ions (C1.3) and no uncancelled electrons (C16.18, C17.21). Multiples are usually allowed.

**Mechanism arrows**
- **Arrows start at a bond or a lone pair.** HBr + propene is worth 6 marks: arrow from C=C to Hδ+, the H–Br dipole with an arrow to Br, the secondary carbocation, the lone pair on Br⁻ with an arrow to C⁺, the name of the mechanism, and the name of the product (C5.6).
- **Nucleophilic substitution:** the C–X dipole, the lone pair and charge on OH⁻, and two arrows. An SN1 route for a primary halogenoalkane earns only the first mark (C10.9).
- **Electrophilic substitution:** the equation that makes the electrophile, an arrow from the ring, the horseshoe intermediate, and an arrow from C–H back into the ring (C18.5).
- **Free radical:** name each stage, state homolytic fission, and give initiation, two propagation steps and one termination (C4.18). The dot is "an unpaired electron", and a missing dot is penalised (C4.7).

**Definitions, explanations and QWC**
- **Key phrases score.** Ionisation energy is "per mole" of "gaseous" atoms (C2.8). A buffer resists pH change (1 mark) when small amounts of acid or alkali are added (1 mark) (C14.17).
- **Explanations are linked chains,** and some marks depend on the one before (C8.20, C10.12, C12.3). The colour of complexes is a four-link chain: 3d orbitals split by ligands, an electron is promoted, light is absorbed, the complementary colour is seen. Leaving out "3d" is penalised (C17.8).
- **Starred and QWC items** are marked against indicative points (C9.11, C18.3), sometimes with marks for structure and reasoning (C17.29). Make each point explicitly.
- **Vague words fail.** "Electrons free to move" (C18.2), plain "blue" for deep blue (P6.analysis), "gas forms" (P3.implementation), and "hexanol" with no number (C10.15) all lose marks.

**Observations, reagents and conditions**
- **Give the start and end colours:** "both colours needed" (C5.5). Accepted shades vary between papers, so learn the standard colours exactly.
- **Concentration and temperature count.** Tin with concentrated HCl ("dilute HCl rejected") and nitration at 50–60 °C (C19.5); diazotisation at 0–10 °C (C19.6); KCN in ethanol, "not HCN" (C20.2); distil for the aldehyde and reflux for the acid (C10.18).
- **Formulae need charges.** [CuCl₄]²⁻ needs its charge (P6.analysis), and mass-spectrum fragments need the "+" (C15.17).

**Answer discipline**
- **Extra answers cost marks.** The list principle (C1.11); "max 1 if more than three are drawn" (C4.11); extra oxidation-number changes are penalised (C8.5); marking extra chiral carbons scores 0 (C15.1).
- **Safety:** the precaution must match the hazard. Goggles, lab coats and fume cupboards are often excluded (P3.planning, P6.implementation).
- **Polymers:** draw two repeat units with continuation bonds. Brackets and n are ignored (C5.7, C15.16).

**"Show that"** (11 items). The example in the notes (C14.10: the pH of water at 373 K is 6.13) credits the route: [H⁺] = √Kw = 7.416 × 10⁻⁷, then pH. The answer is given, so the marks are for the steps. Write every step and carry more figures than the target.

**Unit 3 (WCH13)**
- **Implementation:** test-tube analysis with observations and equations (Ba²⁺, CO₃²⁻, Cl⁻); organic tests, such as dichromate turning orange to green and two observations for sodium with an alcohol.
- **Results:** titration chains with ecf; identifying a compound from IR, backed by two wavenumber ranges; percentage uncertainty with the burette error doubled (2 × 0.05/40.00 × 100 = 0.25%); improvements that go beyond "repeat the titration".
- **Planning:** making an enthalpy experiment better (powdered solid, stirring, extrapolating the cooling line); a hazard with a matched precaution; gas-collection diagrams (syringe or inverted burette, no gaps).

**Unit 6 (WCH16)**
- **Analysis:** identifying transition-metal ions (green Fe(OH)₂ turning brown; manganate(VII) decolourised), complex-ion formulae with charges, and the copper–ammonia observation sequence.
- **Implementation:** quenching kinetics samples, choosing an indicator from the steep section of the curve, and matched safety precautions.
- **Planning:** another way to follow a reaction (colorimetry, conductivity, titration) with a reason; reagent lists (concentrated HNO₃ and H₂SO₄); hazard symbols.

The written papers test the same skills (C6.5, C6.8, C8.20, C8.22, C11.3, C11.10, C16.15, C16.16, C20.5), so revise practical work as one toolkit.

## 6. Topic-by-topic revision priorities

Each block gives the topic's rank by marks from Section 2.3. "Low" still includes points examined every year. It means a lower return per hour of revision, not "skip".

### Must-know

**T1 Formulae, equations and amount of substance (Unit 1): 450 items, 952 marks, rank 1.**
- **Key points:** C1.8 gas volumes (85 items), C1.7 reacting masses (52), C1.6 empirical and molecular formulae (49). Seven of the topic's 12 points are in the top 25.
- **Master:** moles from mass, gas volume (24 dm³ mol⁻¹) and concentration; limiting reagents; yield versus atom economy; ppm.
- **Trap:** count every gas left over, including unreacted reactant. 500 cm³ NH₃ that is 20% decomposed gives 600 cm³.

**T15 Carbonyls, carboxylic acids, chirality, spectroscopy and chromatography (Unit 4): 396 items, 871 marks, rank 2.**
- **Key points:** C15.8 carbonyl tests (99 items, the most frequent content point), C15.20 proton NMR (45), C15.12 carboxylic acid reactions (39).
- **Master:** each test scores the reagent plus the result, tied to the right compound. Fehling's/Benedict's gives a red precipitate, Tollens' a silver mirror, 2,4-DNPH a yellow-orange precipitate, and iodoform a pale yellow precipitate from CH₃CO. ¹³C NMR (C15.19) appears only in 2020–2024.
- **Traps:** marking extra chiral carbons scores 0. Attack on a planar C=O gives a racemic mixture.

**T8 Redox chemistry and Groups 1, 2 and 7 (Unit 2): 322 items, 675 marks, rank 3.**
- **Key points:** C8.2 oxidation numbers (41), C8.20 titration calculations (25 items, 120 marks), C8.16 thermal stability (25). C8.5, C8.27 and C8.7 have 23–26 items each.
- **Master:** signed oxidation numbers; disproportionation; thermal stability through cation charge density and anion polarisation; iodide reducing H₂SO₄ to H₂S (−2); starch added near the end-point.

**T10 Halogenoalkanes, alcohols, mass spectra and IR (Unit 2): 262 items, 599 marks, rank 4.**
- **Key points:** C10.22 IR (50, including greenhouse-gas items), C10.18 dichromate oxidation (33), C10.9 nucleophilic substitution (31 items, 114 marks).
- **Master:** aqueous OH⁻ substitutes and alcoholic KOH eliminates; distil for the aldehyde and reflux for the acid (orange to green); AgCl is white, AgBr cream and AgI yellow.
- **Trap:** an SN1 mechanism for a primary halogenoalkane scores one mark.

### High

**T2 Atomic structure and the Periodic Table (Unit 1): 238 items, 541 marks, rank 5.**
- **Key points:** C2.6 mass spectrometry (41), C2.14 configurations (36), C2.10 ionisation energy factors (34).
- **Master:** Ar is the weighted mean of the peaks; Cl₂ gives five peaks (35, 37, 70, 72, 74); S has a lower first ionisation energy than P because of spin-pair repulsion in 3p; a big jump in successive ionisation energies shows the group. Ionisation energy equations need gaseous state symbols.

**T3 Bonding and structure (Unit 1): 259 items, 508 marks, rank 6.**
- **Why it ranks here:** none of its points is in the top 25. Its marks are spread over 21 points, and it has the lowest marks per item of any topic (1.96).
- **Key points:** C3.11 dot-and-cross diagrams, including dative bonds (29), C3.19 shapes (27), C3.15 polar molecules (25, all one-mark).
- **Master:** shape plus angle plus reason (electron pairs repel to minimise repulsion); lone-pair effects (H₃O⁺ is 107°, XeF₄ is square planar); radii of isoelectronic ions.

**T17 Transition metals (Unit 5): 218 items, 502 marks, rank 7.**
- **Key points:** the marks are spread over 31 points. The main ones are C17.2 configurations (19), C17.22 metal-ion reactions (18) and C17.16 multidentate ligands (18).
- **Biggest per item:** C17.8 colour (9 items, 50 marks), C17.20 dichromate (6, 40), C17.19 vanadium (6, 36).
- **Master:** Cr is 3d⁵4s¹; Zn²⁺ and Ti⁴⁺ are colourless; zinc and chromium hydroxides are amphoteric; the chelate effect means more particles and a positive ΔS system; vanadium colours are V(V) yellow, V(IV) blue, V(III) green and V(II) purple.

**T12 Entropy and energetics (Unit 4): 163 items, 499 marks, rank 8.**
- **Key points:** C12.13 Born–Haber cycles (27), C12.8 ΔS surroundings and total (18 items, 120 marks), C12.17 enthalpy of solution (18).
- **Master:** ΔS surroundings = −ΔH/T with ΔH in J; ΔS total = 0 gives the minimum temperature; MCl₂ needs two electron affinities; ΔH solution = −lattice energy + hydration enthalpies; a Born–Haber value more exothermic than theory means covalent character.

**T4 Introductory organic chemistry and alkanes (Unit 1): 216 items, 496 marks, rank 9.**
- **Key points:** C4.18 free-radical substitution (47 items, 159 marks), C4.12 fuels and cracking (35), C4.10 structural isomers (23).
- **Master:** the full mechanism write-up (UV, homolytic fission, initiation, two propagation steps, termination); Cl• destroying ozone; balanced cracking equations; naming isomers.

**T5 Alkenes (Unit 1): 186 items, 485 marks, rank 10.**
- **Key points:** C5.7 addition polymers (40), C5.4 alkene reactions (34), C5.6 electrophilic addition (33 items, 149 marks). All three appear every year.
- **Master:** the major product comes from the secondary carbocation; KMnO₄ gives a diol (purple to colourless); bromine water is decolourised; E/Z isomerism needs restricted rotation and two different groups on each C=C carbon (C5.2, C5.3).

**T16 Redox equilibria (Unit 5): 170 items, 484 marks, rank 11.**
- **Key points:** C16.15 redox titrations (31 items, 196 marks, the most marks of any Unit 5 point), C16.5 measuring E (20), C16.7 Ecell (16).
- **Master:** back-titration chains (thiosulfate/iodine, manganate(VII)/Fe²⁺); the hydrogen electrode (1 mol dm⁻³ H⁺, H₂ at 100 kPa/1 atm, Pt, 298 K); Pt plus both ions for ion/ion couples; never multiplying E by the stoichiometry; a positive Ecell can still be blocked by kinetics (C16.12).

**T14 Acid–base equilibria (Unit 4): 164 items, 468 marks, rank 12.**
- **Key points:** C14.15 titration curves (22), C14.16 indicators (19), C14.9 weak-acid pH (18 items, 95 marks). C14.19 buffers adds 13 items and 57 marks.
- **Master:** [H⁺] = √(Ka × c) with both assumptions stated; strong-base pH via Kw (12.7, not 13); buffer [H⁺] = Ka × [acid]/[salt]; pH = pKa at half-neutralisation; water stays neutral when Kw changes.

**T11 Kinetics (Unit 4): 143 items, 438 marks, rank 13.**

## 7. Suggested study plan

**How to practise any topic**
1. **Start with its multiple-choice items.** They are 58% of all items, so they cover a topic quickly and show you the gaps.
2. **Then do the structured items for its key points.** Mark them against the exact mark-scheme wording.
3. **Work each "hence" bundle as one piece** (there are 79). Never practise part (ii) on its own. If an item lists prerequisites (200 do), revise those points first.
4. **Keep an error log by rule:** sign, units, significant figures, state symbols, vague wording, extra answers.

**Order of study**
1. **Moles toolkit (T1),** then the titration chains built on it (C8.20, C16.15, P3.results). This is the highest-mark topic, and it feeds every calculation chain in Section 3.
2. **Core organic (T15, T10, T4, T5).** Make one sheet of reagents, conditions and observations from C15.8, C15.12, C15.14, C10.8, C10.18 and C5.4. Practise drawing the four mechanisms (C4.18, C5.6, C10.9, C18.5) from memory, checking against the Section 4 rules.
3. **Redox and inorganic (T8, then T16, then T17).** Learn oxidation numbers and half-equations before electrode potentials. Then do vanadium and chromium (C17.19, C17.20) and metal-ion observations (C17.22, P6.analysis).
4. **Physical chemistry chains (T12, T14, T11, T13, T6).** Work each chain from start to finish:
   - ΔS system → ΔS surroundings → ΔS total → minimum temperature;
   - Kc expression → value → units;
   - Ka → pH → buffer → titration curve;
   - orders → rate equation → k → Ea;
   - Q = mcΔT → ΔH.
5. **Structure and properties (T2, T3, T9, T7).** These are mostly short items. Practise shapes, ionisation energy explanations, Le Chatelier and boiling-point comparisons in short sessions.
6. **A2 organic to finish (T18, T19, then T20).** Do synthesis last: it reuses everything else and pays about 4 marks per item.

**Throughout:** do one P3 or P6 set each week.
**In the final weeks:** sit timed papers unit by unit, then make one pass over the rarely tested list (Section 2.2).
**If you are taking only the AS:** your whole scope is Units 1–3, which means Topics 1–10 and P3.

## 8. Data caveats

- **Older papers were mapped by closest match.** WCH01–WCH07 questions were placed on the nearest 2018 spec point, so counts from older papers are approximate. Three items did not fit any point (OFF).
- **Two scanned papers could not be read:** WCH06 January 2019 and WCH03 June 2019. Units 6 and 3 are each one 2019 paper short.
- **Some mark schemes were missing, belonged to another paper, or were examiners' reports.** Notes marked "key absent", "derived" or "no MS in input" were worked out rather than read from a scheme. Treat them as likely, not confirmed.
- **Chemical formulae lost subscripts and charges** in the source text (for example "SO4 2-"). Some diagrams and multiple-choice options were garbled, and a few answer letters are unknown.
- **A few questions were split imperfectly.** Some items hold a whole question (C1.11 is one item worth 13 marks), and others show only part of the question text.
- **"Multiple choice" here means one-mark items.** Some one-mark written answers are counted in it, so 58% is an upper limit.
- **Command words come from paraphrased summaries,** using the first match only, and 1,978 items matched none.
- **Shares describe the bank, not official weightings.** The data has no papers-per-unit or marks-per-paper figures. 2014–2016 had fewer papers (12, 12 and 10), so raw counts by year favour later years. "Years seen" is a fairer measure of how regularly a point comes up.
- **Topic totals leave out practical-coded items,** even though P3/P6 items also test content.
- **There are only three sample notes per spec point,** so Section 4 shows patterns rather than a full survey of every mark scheme.
- **2025 papers are excluded.**
