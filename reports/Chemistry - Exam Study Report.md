# Chemistry: Exam Study Report

Pearson Edexcel International A Level Chemistry: International AS units WCH11–WCH13, A2 units WCH14–WCH16, plus the older WCH01–WCH07 papers. Built from a topic-sorted bank of 4,794 past-paper question parts, sessions 2014–2024 (2025 papers are deliberately left out). Every figure comes from the bank's statistics file or is calculated directly from it.

## What the paper codes mean

Pearson gives every paper a code such as **WCH11**.

- **W** = International Advanced Level paper; **CH** = Chemistry.
- **First digit** = specification: **1** = current 2018 specification (WCH11–WCH16); **0** = previous 2013 specification (WCH01–WCH07).
- **Last digit** = unit number. Units 1–3 are International AS, units 4–6 are A2, and unit 7 (2017 only) was a practical examination.

| Unit | Name | Level |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Structure, Bonding and Introduction to Organic Chemistry | AS |
| 2 | Energetics, Group Chemistry, Halogenoalkanes and Analytical Techniques | AS |
| 3 | Practical Skills in Chemistry I | AS |
| 4 | Rates, Equilibria and Further Organic Chemistry | A2 |
| 5 | Transition Metals and Organic Nitrogen Chemistry | A2 |
| 6 | Practical Skills in Chemistry II | A2 |

An item ID such as **WCH12_Jun2021-Q17-a** reads: paper code (WCH12, current-spec Unit 2), session (June 2021), question 17, part (a).

Spec codes such as **C6.5** mean topic 6, point 5 of the 2018 specification (20 topics across Units 1, 2, 4 and 5). **P3.\*** and **P6.\*** are practical-skills codes (Units 3 and 6), **G** is general skills, and **OFF** is old-spec content that is not in the 2018 specification.

### 3.2 One-mark items

2,697 items (56%) are worth one mark. Many are not simple recall: the notes show multiple-choice calculations of gas volumes (C1.8), yield (C1.9), empirical formulae (C1.6), Kp (C13.3), Born-Haber terms (C12.13), enthalpy of solution (C12.17), E°cell (C16.7) and buffer pH (C14.19). There is no credit for working in these, so speed and accuracy both matter.

### 3.3 Typical question structures

Most written questions in the notes take one of six shapes:

1. **Calculation chain.** (i) moles of one substance, (ii) hence moles of another, (iii) mass, concentration or volume, often to 3 s.f. Examples: antacid-tablet back titration (P3.results), iodine/thiosulfate back titration (C16.15), spirit-burner enthalpy (C6.5), ΔS system → ΔS surroundings → ΔS total → feasibility (C12.8).
2. **Mechanism.** Name it, draw curly arrows, dipoles, lone pairs and the intermediate, name the product (C5.6 up to 6 marks; C4.18 up to 7).
3. **Reaction scheme or synthesis.** Reagents and conditions for each step, plus intermediates (C20.2, C20.3, C19.4, C19.5, C5.4).
4. **Test and observation.** A reagent and the result for each compound, often with an ionic equation (C15.8, C8.19, C17.22).
5. **Structure from data.** Combine IR, mass spectrum, NMR and chemical tests (C20.1, C20.3, C15.17, C10.21).
6. **Explain a trend.** Ionisation energy, thermal stability, lattice energy, boiling temperature (C2.10, C8.16, C12.18, C7.5).

### 3.4 "Hence" bundles and prerequisites

The bank keeps a "hence" part with the part it depends on, so the pair counts as one item: 83 such bundles, and 219 items list prerequisites. The data does not say which items these are, but the notes show the usual shape: "calculate ΔS surroundings, and hence ΔS total" (C12.8); "draw the dot-and-cross diagram of SCN⁻ and hence its structure" (C17.24); "decide which acid is stronger, hence complete the equation" (C14.8).

The notes keep linking the same chains of ideas, so learn them in order:

- **Moles first.** The high-mark calculations (C8.20, C16.15, C6.5, C20.1) all start from Topic 1.
- **Energy cycles.** Hess's law (C6.6) → Born-Haber (C12.13) → enthalpy of solution (C12.17).
- **Entropy.** ΔS system (C12.7) → ΔS surroundings and total (C12.8) → feasibility (C12.9) → ΔS total = R lnK (C13.8) → E°cell proportional to ΔS total and lnK (C16.11).
- **Substitution.** Nucleophilic substitution (C10.9) → SN1/SN2 rate evidence (C11.9) → optical-activity evidence (C15.5).

### 3.5 Starred extended-writing parts

The bank makes each starred part its own item, but the data does not count them. The notes show two kinds:

- **Point-marked explanations**, usually 2–4 marks, one mark per correct point: why sulfur's first ionisation energy is below phosphorus's (C2.14), why magnesium's second ionisation energy is greater (C2.8), silicon vs phosphorus melting temperature (C2.18), flame-test transitions (C8.17), lattice energies of NaF vs MgF₂ (C12.18), why a complex ion is coloured (C17.8). Some are longer: the free-radical mechanism (C4.18, 7 marks) and alkane vs alcohol volatility (C7.5, marked as "seven one-mark points rather than a level grid").
- **Six-mark level-of-response answers**: conditions for ethanol hydration (C9.11), optical activity of muscle vs laboratory lactic acid (C15.3), catalytic converter vs V₂O₅ catalysis (C17.29), bromine with benzene, cyclohexene and phenol (C18.3).

Starred parts turn up across almost every topic, including Topic 1 (C1.3), energetics (C6.6, C6.8) and practical-style parts (C15.22, C20.4).

## 4. What mark schemes reward

### 4.1 Calculations

- **Each step is a marking point**, and error carried forward is allowed: a wrong earlier answer used correctly later still scores (C1.7, C1.9, C6.6, P3.results, C20.1).
- **A correct final answer alone** often scores full marks (C14.11: "correct answer alone scores 2"; C16.9), but only if it is right, so show working.
- **Watch the factors**: two chloride ions in CaCl₂ (C12.17); do not multiply E° by the equation coefficients (C16.7); the factor 2 in ΔH when the equation uses 2 mol (C6.5); I₂ : S₂O₃²⁻ is 1 : 2 (C16.15); burette uncertainty is doubled for two readings, pipette uncertainty is not (C8.22).
- **Equilibrium expressions**: leave out solids, square [OH⁻] rather than doubling it (C13.1); Kp needs p terms ("square brackets and expressions without p are rejected", C13.2); halving an equation square-roots K.

### 4.2 Signs, units and significant figures

- **Signs**: ΔH is marked "value, then sign and 3 s.f." (C6.5: −495 kJ mol⁻¹), and "+23 kJ mol⁻¹, + sign for endothermic". A bond enthalpy of −347 instead of +347 scores 1 of 2 (C6.10). An oxidation number written as "2" without a sign is not accepted (C8.2).
- **Units**: ΔS in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ with its sign (C12.7, C12.8); convert kJ to J before dividing ΔH by T (C12.8: 92 200 / 298); units of Kc (C13.3), k (C11.1) and Ka (C14.14, where units are penalised).
- **Significant figures**: 3 s.f. is demanded in many final answers (P3.results 0.417 g; C4.10 67.1%; C11.10 Ea; C16.15 SO₂ in mg dm⁻³). Use 2 s.f. when the data is 2 s.f. (C6.5, +23). pH 12.7 is accepted but 13 is rejected (C14.11). Log values were asked to 2 d.p. (C2.17).

### 4.3 Equations and state symbols

- The usual split is one mark for correct species and one for balancing plus state symbols (C10.17, C8.19). "No or wrong state symbols max 1, wrong balancing max 1" (C4.17).
- The state-symbol mark may count only if the equation is right (C8.14). If the question says state symbols are not required, they are ignored (C15.12).
- Ionic equations: remove spectator ions, keep solids as formulae: CaCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) (C1.3).
- Ionisation-energy equations need gaseous species: Mg⁺(g) → Mg²⁺(g) + e⁻ (C2.8).
- Combining half-equations: electrons must cancel; leftover electrons, H⁺ or H₂O lose the mark (C8.10, C16.18, C17.21). Multiples are allowed.
- Charges matter: [CuCl₄]²⁻ written without its charge is not accepted (P6.analysis); mass-spectrum fragments need the + charge (C15.17).

### 4.4 Mechanism arrows

- **Electrophilic addition (C5.6)**, six marks in one example: arrow from C=C to Hδ+; H–Br dipole and arrow to Br; correct (secondary) carbocation; lone pair on Br⁻ with arrow to C⁺; the name "electrophilic addition"; the product name.
- **Nucleophilic substitution (C10.9)**: Cδ+–Xδ− dipole; lone pair and negative charge on OH⁻; arrow from the lone pair to C; arrow from the C–X bond to X. An SN1 route here scores only part of the marks.
- **Electrophilic substitution (C18.5)**: an equation making the electrophile (e.g. CH₃X + AlCl₃ → CH₃⁺ + AlCl₃X⁻); arrow from the ring to the electrophile; horseshoe intermediate carrying H and the new group; arrow from the C–H bond back into the ring.
- **Free-radical substitution (C4.18)**: name the mechanism and homolytic fission; initiation, two propagation steps, one termination step. Every radical needs its dot ("omitted dot penalised once", C4.7), and the dot means "an unpaired electron", not "a free electron".
- **SN1 and optical activity (C11.9, C15.5)**: a planar carbocation attacked from either side gives a racemic mixture; SN2 gives a single optically active product.

### 4.5 Level-of-response and QWC

- Six-mark level-of-response answers split into **4 marks for indicative content and 2 for structure and linked reasoning** (C9.11, C15.3).
- Many starred parts are point-marked instead, one mark per correct point (C17.8, C7.5, C7.1).
- Order can carry a mark: in a crystallisation method, "last mark only if the sequence is right" (G).
- Calculations can carry QWC too: a 6-mark combustion-analysis calculation gave "1 mark each step" for any correct method (C20.1).
- The full level descriptors are not in the data. The notes do show that marks go to specific chemical points linked cause → effect, so write one point per sentence and tie each to the question.

### 4.6 "Show that" questions

Only 11 items start with "Show that", so the data supports few rules. In the one example in the notes (C14.10, show that the pH of water at 373 K is 6.13), the marks are for the working: [H⁺] = √Kw = 7.416 × 10⁻⁷, then pH = 6.13 (or pH = ½pKw). Since the answer is given, write every step and keep more figures in intermediate values than in the target. The same idea applies to "use oxidation numbers to show…" (C8.5): give both changes (Cl −1 → 0, Mn +7 → +2); extra changes are penalised.

### 4.7 Words that win or lose marks

- **Precise observations**: "deep/dark/royal blue", not just "blue" (P6.analysis); liquid bromine goes "red-brown to colourless", and orange or yellow is rejected (C5.5); "gas forms" alone is not enough, say effervescence (P3.implementation).
- **Linked answers**: reagent and result must be matched to the right compound (C15.8); each hazard must be matched to its precaution (P6.implementation); an indicator's colour change only scores if the indicator is right (C8.20).
- **Complete names**: "hexanol" alone is rejected (C10.15); "fractional distillation", "ligand exchange" and "delocalised electrons" need both words (C4.12, C17.24, C3.20).
- **No over-answering**: drawing more than three isomers when three are asked caps the mark (C4.11); marking more than one carbon as the chiral centre scores nothing (C15.1).
- **Generic answers score nothing**: goggles, lab coat, fume cupboard (P3.planning, P6.implementation); "repeat the titration" (P3.results); "heat loss" when another reason is asked for (C6.5, C6.8).
- **Definitions need every key phrase**: first ionisation energy "per mole" and "gaseous" (C2.8); a buffer "resists change in pH" when "small amounts of acid or alkali" are added (C14.17).

**T1 Formulae, equations and amount of substance** (Unit 1 · 458 items · 970 marks · rank 1)

## 8. Data caveats

- **Older papers were mapped to the closest 2018 point.** WCH01–WCH07 questions sit on the nearest 2018 spec point, so some counts include content the 2018 wording does not name. Examples in the notes: greenhouse-gas multiple choice coded C10.22 (infrared), ppm conversions coded C1.5, and four OFF items (alcohol taxation, combinatorial chemistry, polymer supports).
- **Two scanned papers could not be read**: WCH06 January 2019 and WCH03 June 2019. Their items are missing, so Units 3 and 6 are slightly under-counted.
- **Some mark schemes were missing, belonged to another paper, or were examiners' reports.** The notes flag these ("Mark scheme not in input", "Answer key not available", "Derived", "By working (unconfirmed)"), and the expected answer was inferred. Treat those notes as guidance, not the official scheme.
- **Chemical formulae lost subscripts and charges** in the source text (for example "SO4 2-", and isotope superscripts in a C2.6 item whose answer mapping had to be inferred). Formulae in this report are written out normally.
- **A few questions were split imperfectly.** Some items cover a whole multi-part question (C1.11 is a single 13-mark item; one C3.5 item is noted as "whole question is one item"), and some notes describe parts that appear only in the mark scheme.
- **Each item has one main spec code.** The per-point counts add up exactly to 4,794, so content tested as a secondary code (such as a C-point inside a practical question) is not counted under that point. This is one likely reason core practicals show zero items.
- **Command words** are the first match per item and were found in only 2,824 items.
- **Starred items are not counted** in the data, and full level-of-response descriptors are not included.
- **No per-paper figures.** The data gives no papers-per-unit or paper mark totals, so averages per paper, grade boundaries and timings cannot be worked out from it.
- **Year counts are not trends.** Papers per year vary from 10 to 20, and 2025 papers are deliberately excluded.
